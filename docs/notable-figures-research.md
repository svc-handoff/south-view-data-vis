# Notable figures: research notes

The 30 entries in `src/data/individuals.json` are real people buried at South-View Cemetery. Each has a
short blurb and at least three sources, listed in the app's figure dialog: D L Henderson, *South-View: An
African American City of the Dead* (Carrelspin Press, 2018) first, then archival records, newspaper
coverage, or scholarly and institutional sources. No Wikipedia, Find a Grave, or other wikis are cited.

## How this was built

- The earlier entries were fictional placeholders. Eight were modelled on real people (the Herndons, the
  Dobbses, Rev. E. R. Carter, and Dr. Henry R. and Selena Sloan Butler); the real people replace them, except
  the Butlers (see below). The rest were replaced with people Henderson documents as buried at South-View.
- Research agents read Henderson for each person, then found and opened every other cited source. Every
  non-Henderson link was re-checked with a plain HTTP request: 75 of 77 returned 200. The UPI archive story
  (King Sr.) and the Library of Congress item page (David T. Howard) refuse automated requests; the Library of
  Congress record was confirmed through its official JSON API.
- Some Georgia Historic Newspapers pages (Atlanta Daily World and 19th-century papers) show a browser check to
  automated tools, so their text was read through the site's own search API. The cited links are the normal
  public page links, which open in a browser.
- Birth years are left blank (shown as "d. 1945") when no opened source gave one.

## Ruled out

- **Dr. Henry Rutherford Butler and Selena Sloan Butler**: buried at Oakland Cemetery, not South-View
  (Henderson's notes; New Georgia Encyclopedia).
- **Julian Bond**: Henderson lists a South-View marker, but the *Atlanta Journal-Constitution* (Aug. 18, 2015)
  reported his ashes were scattered in the Gulf of Mexico, so the marker may be a memorial.
- **Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., Dr. Benjamin E. Mays, Lt. Henry O. Flipper**: first buried at South-View, later
  moved (Henderson, ch. 1).
- **Henry A. Rucker**: not in Henderson, and no acceptable source confirms a South-View burial.
- **Mary L. Bentley**: Henderson does not say she is buried at South-View; no other source found.
- **Ben H. Townsley** and **William J. "Bill" Shaw**: researched and confirmed, but left out as the two
  weakest-sourced of 32 candidates (drafts kept outside the repo).

### Charles Henry Morgan (?–1886)

**Burial at South-View confirmed by:** Henderson ch. 3 ("He died only days later and was buried at South-View on December 17, 1886," citing South-View burial records) and Georgia Tech Building Memories ("one of the first citizens to be buried in the cemetery," December 17, 1886).

**Source checks:** Sentence 1: South Carolina native, carriage trimmer, grocer: Henderson ch. 3; Georgia Tech (carriage trimmer, successful grocer); Carter's The Black Side (1894), p. 62, says Crumbly after leaving the army 'was employed by Mr. Charles H. Morgan, who was at that time a prosperous grocer.' Sentence 2: charter petition signer: Henderson ch. 3 "The Charter"; Georgia Tech; ADW 1946 lists C. H. Morgan among the organizers. Three shares bought December 6, 1886, and burial December 17, 1886: Henderson ch. 3 (citing estate appraisal and South-View burial records); Georgia Tech gives the December 17, 1886 burial. Disagreement: Henderson says Morgan bought 'the first three shares' of South-View stock, but ADW 1946 says 'The first share of stock was sold to J. T. Schell'; Henderson's endnote 7 explains that Schell bought Certificate no. 1, originally held by Morgan. The bio therefore says only that he bought three shares on December 6, 1886. Birth year: not given by any source I found (left null). Death: December 1886 (Henderson: 'only days' after December 6; buried December 17). Henderson and Georgia Tech also say he was an Atlanta University trustee, and Henderson that he helped found First Congregational Church; not used in the bio. Georgia Historic Newspapers page views sit behind a browser-verification screen, so WebFetch returned only that screen; the page's OCR text was opened and read through the archive's own JSON search endpoint (gahistoricnewspapers.galileo.usg.edu/search/pages/results/?...&format=json), which returns the same page id.

### George Wesley Graham (1833–1894)

**Burial at South-View confirmed by:** Henderson only, and indirectly: ch. 3 prints a photograph captioned "Family lot of South-View founder George Graham" showing a GRAHAM monument at South-View, and ch. 15 records that his widow Harriet was buried at South-View in 1904. Henderson's text does not state Graham's own interment in so many words, and no other source I could open confirms it.

**Source checks:** Sentence 1: carpenter, elected as one of the first two African Americans on the Atlanta City Council, Third Ward: Henderson ch. 3 ("In 1871, thirty-eight-year-old George Wesley Graham, a carpenter, won election..."); Atlanta History Center ("Finch and fellow Black Republican George Graham became the first two African Americans elected to city council"); Bacote 1959 (election of December 7, 1870; "George Graham, was elected from the Third Ward"; Graham and Finch left office January 5, 1872); Daily Atlanta Intelligencer Jan. 31, 1871 lists George Graham "(negro)" as a Third Ward councilman under "Municipal Government for 1871" and puts him on the Wells, Pumps and Printing committees, matching Henderson. Georgia Tech also says he served one year for the old Third Ward. Date note: the election was held December 7, 1870 for the 1871 council term; Henderson phrases it as winning election 'in 1871'. The bio gives the election date and the service year to avoid the ambiguity. Sentence 2: grocery and wood/coal business: Henderson ch. 3 ("he opened a grocery business in the mid-1880s. He also sold wood and coal"). Charter petition signer: Henderson ch. 3 "The Charter"; Georgia Tech lists him among the six signers; ADW 1946 lists George W. Graham among the organizers. Dates: birth year 1833 is approximate, derived from Henderson calling him thirty-eight years old at his 1871 election (no source gives an exact birth year). Death 1894: Henderson cites his Atlanta Constitution obituary of September 5, 1894 (not opened; ProQuest-only). ### Adrienne McNeil Herndon (1869–1910)

**Burial at South-View confirmed by:** Henderson ch. 6 "Herndon: Simplicity of Design": family members interred on the Herndon lot at South-View include "Herndon's first wife, educator and actress Adrienne McNeil (d. 1910)." No second online source states her burial place; burial_year 1910 is inferred from her death year.

**Source checks:** Sentence 1: born in Savannah, Atlanta University graduate, taught elocution and staged Shakespearean productions (Henderson dictionary entry); head of drama and elocution at Atlanta University and presented the university's first Shakespearean production, The Merchant of Venice, in 1905 (Southern Spaces); professor at Atlanta University (NGE; Atlanta History Center). Sentence 2: January 1904 Boston debut at Steinert Hall under the stage name Anne Du Bignon (Southern Spaces; Henderson dictionary entry); she was the architect of the family house built next to Atlanta University and died of Addison's disease within three months of its completion (Southern Spaces; Henderson calls it "Diamond Hill" and notes its 2000 National Historic Landmark designation). Married Alonzo Herndon in 1893; Norris was their only child (NGE). Dates 1869-1910 from Southern Spaces; Henderson gives d. 1910. No disagreements. Note: Henderson describes the 1904 debut and says she "would never become a working actor"; the Atlanta History Center page says she performed on stages in New York and Boston; the blurb uses only the sourced Boston debut.

### Bishop Henry McNeal Turner (1834–1915)

**Burial at South-View confirmed by:** Henderson ch. 12 (his ledger stone at South-View, placed in 1996, and the 1915 funeral procession "to his gravesite at South-View") and the Georgia Historical Society marker, which lists "Bishop Henry McNeal Turner, Reconstruction-era state legislator and African Methodist Episcopal Church organizer" among South-View's notable burials. NGE and the South Carolina Encyclopedia say only that he was buried in Atlanta.

**Source checks:** Sentence 1: born free in South Carolina (NGE: Newberry Courthouse, "never enslaved"; SC Encyclopedia; Henderson); Union Army chaplain of the 1st U.S. Colored Troops from 1863 (NGE; Henderson); organized the AME Church in Georgia after the war (NGE; GHS marker "African Methodist Episcopal Church organizer"); elected to the Georgia House from Macon and expelled with other Black members in 1868 (NGE; Henderson). Sentence 2: elected AME bishop in 1880 (SC Encyclopedia; NGE; Henderson); emigration advocacy, International Migration Society, Liberia voyages 1895-96 (NGE; Henderson); died May 8, 1915 in Windsor, Ontario (NGE; SC Encyclopedia); an estimated 25,000 mourners at Big Bethel AME Church on May 19, 1915 (Henderson ch. 12 only). Birth year disagreement: NGE and the SC Encyclopedia text give 1834 (SC Encyclopedia: February 1, 1834, though its page header misprints "1843"); Henderson says he was born in 1831 and lived eighty-three years. Used 1834 from the two scholarly encyclopedias (both by or citing historian Stephen W. Angell). Relation: Turner wrote the introduction to Edward R. Carter's The Black Side (DLG/Emory record; Henderson notes).

### Moses Amos (1866–1928)

**Burial at South-View confirmed by:** Henderson, ch. 13 "Holmes-Amos: Family Tree in Stone": the family ledger stone at South-View lists Moses Amos Sr. (d. 1928) among seven family members buried on the lot; he is also in bold (= buried at South-View) in Henderson's Biographical Dictionary.

**Source checks:** Sentence 1: working at Huss's drugstore as a child, Butler & Slater buying it, Amos becoming manager, and the 1911 license as first African American druggist licensed by the Georgia Pharmacy Board are all from Henderson (ch. 13 and dictionary entry, which says June 1911). SaportaReport independently confirms the Huss apprenticeship, managing the store under Drs. Slater and Butler, and that he is regarded as Georgia's first African American licensed pharmacist; the AARL finding aid calls him 'the first Black pharmacist in Georgia'; APEX calls him the first Black registered pharmacist in Georgia. Sentence 2: 1914 store in the Odd Fellows Building is in Henderson and SaportaReport (grand opening June 1914, Odd Fellows annex at Auburn and Butler St.). Dates 1866-1928 from the AARL finding aid and SaportaReport; Henderson gives d. 1928 only. Disagreement: the year of licensure varies in unvetted web sources (1885-89, 1913); Henderson's 1911 (sourced to his note 11) is used. Henderson calls Butler, Slater & Co. 'the first African American licensed pharmacy in Georgia'; bio says only 'early Black-owned'. The AARL catalog (aafa.galileo.usg.edu) refused connections from here, so the Internet Archive copy of the same finding aid was opened instead. Relations: Herndon (vice president) and David T. Howard (director) were officers/directors of the 1914 Gate City Drugstore company (Henderson ch. 13). Henry Butler would be a colleague but is not buried at South-View, so that link is omitted.

### Rev. Edward R. Carter (1856–1944)

**Burial at South-View confirmed by:** Henderson ch. 13: "The gravesite of ... Rev. Edward Randolph Carter (d. 1944) is covered by a ledger stone" at South-View; Henderson ch. 1 also lists him on the 1961 South-View monument to deceased board members "now resting in this cemetery." No second online source states his burial place.

**Source checks:** Sentence 1: pastor of Friendship Baptist Church "for more than 60 years" (DLG/Auburn Avenue Research Library record) and "sixty-one years" per his ledger stone (Henderson ch. 13); the King Institute record of a 1935 service marking the 53rd anniversary of his ministry at Friendship places the start around 1882. Sentence 2: The Black Side published 1894 by Edward R. Carter, subject Black Atlanta's business, religious and educational history, with an introduction by Bishop H. M. Turner (DLG/Emory record; Henderson ch. 13). Birth year disagreement: the Auburn Avenue Research Library archival record gives 1856-1944; The Black Side itself (1894, p. 244-45) says there is no record of his exact birth date and that he was then "about thirty-five" (c. 1859); library name authorities give "1856 or 1858." Henderson gives only d. 1944. Used 1856 from the archival record. Relations: Carter, Herndon and David T. Howard are all named as South-View directors on the 1961 monument (Henderson ch. 1), and Carter and Howard as original investors; Turner wrote the introduction to The Black Side (DLG record).
