# Suggested changes to the map's historical context

These are **suggestions only**; `src/data/map_snapshots.json` is unchanged. Each suggestion keeps the
current text's facts (from the deeds, the 1944 plat and the Verity Works character-area map) and adds
context from D L Henderson's *South-View: An African American City of the Dead* (2018) and the records
Henderson cites. Every suggestion lists at least three sources; none is Wikipedia.

**Henderson** = D L Henderson, *South-View: An African American City of the Dead* (Dunwoody, GA:
Carrelspin Press, 2018), ISBN 978-0-9985772-0-3 ([WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/search?q=bn:9780998577203)).
**Verity folder** = the "GT - Verity Mapping Folder" (1944 plat, Fulton County deed-book pages, and the
Verity Works character-area map from the 2025 National Register nomination draft).

---

## 1888

**Current:** On May 2, 1888, the South View Cemetery Association bought the original part of the cemetery
on the east side of Jonesboro Road. This section stayed in use until about 1991.

**Issue:** "until about 1991" comes from the Verity map's label for the **Public Grounds** (1888–c.1991),
not the original tract as a whole; the oldest graves are in a separate area labelled "1886–1944 Oldest
Section". The founding (1886) is also missing.

**Suggested:** Chartered in April 1886 by six formerly enslaved Black Atlantans, South-View received the
deed for its original tract on the east side of Jonesboro Road on May 2, 1888. That February it won the
city's contract to bury Atlanta's Black paupers, in a "City Grounds" section that stayed in use until
about 1991.

**Sources:**
- Henderson, ch. 1, "A Cemetery of Their Own" (charter granted April 26, 1886, to six formerly enslaved
  men); ch. 4 note 1 (warranty deed dated May 2, 1888, Fulton County Deed Book 131, folio 326); ch. 5,
  "Burying the Poor" (city pauper contract won February 1888; first "City Graves" burial February 12,
  1888), citing Atlanta City Council minutes, May 7, 1888.
- Verity folder: 1944 plat ("Original part of South View Cemetery purchased May 2nd 1888"); character-area
  map ("1888 – c.1991 Public Grounds").
- Georgia Historical Society marker, "South-View Cemetery" (2016): founded 1886 by Black Atlantans
  objecting to segregated burial grounds.
  <https://georgiahistory.com/ghmi_marker_updated/south-view-cemetery/>

> Note: Henderson says the cemetery opened on 25 acres in 1886 (citing the *Atlanta Constitution*, Feb. 20,
> 1886), while the deed for the original tract is dated 1888. "Received the deed" avoids implying nothing
> happened before 1888.

## 1902

**Current:** On December 1, 1902, the Association bought the ~5-acre "South View Addition" running along the
right-of-way of the A&K Railroad. The railroad line was the cemetery's easternmost edge until around 1957.

**Suggested:** On December 1, 1902, the Association bought the ~5-acre "South View Addition" along the A&K
Railroad right-of-way, which stayed the cemetery's eastern edge until 1957. The same year, the city
returned its pauper-burial contract to South-View at a higher price; in 1901 the cemetery had refused to
bury the city's Black poor for less than the $2 a grave paid to the white Westview Cemetery.

**Sources:**
- Verity folder: 1944 plat ("South View Addition purchased Dec. 1st 1902, 5.25 acres", along the A.&K.
  R.R. right-of-way).
- Henderson, ch. 5, "Burying the Poor" ($1.35 per pauper grave in 1900 vs $2 at Westview; $2 bid in 1901
  refused by the city; contract returned "at an advance of price" in 1902).
- *Atlanta Constitution*, "To Bury Paupers Cheaply," March 23, 1901, and "Will Make Report to Council
  Today," September 3, 1902 (as cited by Henderson, ch. 5 notes 16 and 18).

## 1909

**Current:** A second "South View Addition" of ~5 acres, purchased October 21, 1909, extended the grounds
north to a street that was never opened.

**Suggested:** A second ~5-acre "South View Addition," purchased October 21, 1909, extended the grounds
north to a street that was never opened. That year Chestnut Hill Cemetery opened, the first of several new
choices for Black Atlantans, but South-View remained the cemetery of choice for many families.

**Sources:**
- Verity folder: 1944 plat ("South View Addition purchased Oct. 21st 1909, 5.04 acres"; "Not opened" street).
- Henderson, ch. 1, "Twentieth-Century Residents" (Chestnut Hill Cemetery established 1909; South-View
  remained "the cemetery of choice for many Atlanta families").
- Atlanta street maps, 1935–1945 (Verity folder, "Progression Maps"), which still show the cemetery at
  about this extent, between Jonesboro Road and the railroad.

## 1944

**Current:** In 1944 the Association bought lots north of the old grounds, including the corner of
Jonesboro Road and Helena Street from A. H. Watts in April, and filed an August plat applying to use them
for burials. A September covenant with Harry M. Paschal Jr., who owned the houses across Jonesboro Road,
barred burials on that corner until 1956.

**Suggested:** In 1944 the Association bought lots north of the old grounds, including the Jonesboro
Road–Helena Street corner from A. H. Watts, and filed an August plat applying to use them for burials:
since a 1926 Georgia Supreme Court ruling, new cemetery land outside the city needed county approval. A
September covenant with neighbor Harry M. Paschal Jr. barred burials on that corner until 1956. At its
60th anniversary in 1946, South-View counted about 50,000 burials.

**Sources:**
- Verity folder: 1944 plat (title: "…parts of lots I & K for which application is made to be used for
  cemetery purposes", Aug. 1944); Fulton County Deed Book 2127 p. 248 (A. H. Watts, April 6, 1944);
  Deed Book 3111 pp. 636–638 (Paschal quitclaim, March 28, 1956, reciting the September 15, 1944 covenant).
- *Southview Cemetery Ass'n v. Kitchens*, 162 Ga. 322, 133 S.E. 919 (Ga. 1926): using newly bought land
  next to the cemetery fell under the 1910 act requiring county permission.
  <https://hallapproved.com/ga/cases/supreme/1926/5735117/>
- Henderson, ch. 4, "Mid-Twentieth-Century Growth" and "1940s – Sixtieth Anniversary" (expansion
  litigation; about 50,000 burials by 1946), citing William A. Fowlkes, "Historic South-View Cemetery
  Celebrates," *Atlanta Daily World*, April 21, 1946.

> Caution: the same 1946 *Atlanta Daily World* article (via Henderson) puts the cemetery at about 90 acres
> after "fifteen acres" were added. The deeds date the large eastern purchases to 1956–57, so the map's
> grounds series does not reach ~88 acres until 1957. Avoid quoting the 1946 acreage.

## 1957

**Current:** In ~1957 the Association added about 38 acres east of the old railroad line: an abandoned
railroad strip from Amanda F. Watts (1956), ~22 acres from Nick Poulos (January 1957), and the City of
Atlanta's ~13-acre Thomasville School property (June 1957), conveyed for an exchange of property and
$3,500.

**Suggested:** In 1956–57 the Association added about 38 acres east of the old railroad line: an abandoned
railroad strip from Amanda F. Watts, ~22 acres from Nick Poulos, and the City of Atlanta's ~13-acre
Thomasville School property, traded for other land and $3,500. Atlanta's city limits had reached the
cemetery in 1952, and by its 75th anniversary in 1961 South-View had paved its roads and begun laying out
the new land as the William C. Pauley Section (1961–65).

**Sources:**
- Verity folder: Fulton County Deed Book 3255 p. 202 (Watts, Aug. 14, 1956), Deed Book 3231 p. 516
  (Poulos, Jan. 13, 1957, 22.03 acres), Deed Book 3231 p. 233 (City of Atlanta, June 10, 1957, 12.69
  acres, "exchange of property and $3,500").
- Verity Works character-area map, 2025 National Register nomination draft ("1956-1957 William C Pauley
  Section (dev 1961-1965)").
- Henderson, ch. 4, "1950s – Growth and Expansion" (city limits reached the cemetery in 1952; office moved
  from Auburn Avenue to the grounds in 1954) and "1960s – Seventy-Fifth Anniversary" (roads paved, "park and
  semi-park" sections added by 1961).

## 1977

**Current:** The North Section, added around 1977, filled the land between Helena Street, Jonesboro Road
and Schoen Street. It was assembled from house lots bought one by one, such as No. 1 Helena Street (1957),
No. 3 Helena Street (1969, $6,000) and No. 1904 Jonesboro Road (1976). The grounds of the cemetery have
not meaningfully grown since.

**Suggested:** The North Section, added around 1977, filled the land between Helena Street, Jonesboro Road
and Schoen Street, assembled from house lots bought one by one between 1957 and 1976. In 1978 Atlanta's
Urban Design Commission placed South-View on the city's Roster of Historic Sites. The grounds have not
meaningfully grown since, but burials passed 80,000 by 2017, and in 2026 the cemetery joined the Georgia
Register of Historic Places.

**Sources:**
- Verity folder: Fulton County Deed Books 3187 p. 474 (No. 1 Helena St, 1957), 5050 p. 382 (No. 3 Helena
  St, 1969), 5280 p. 100 (No. 5 Helena St, 1970), 6499 p. 384 (No. 1904 Jonesboro Rd, 1976); character-area
  map ("1977 North Section").
- Henderson, ch. 4, "1970s – Ninetieth Anniversary" (1978 Roster of Historic Sites, citing a letter from
  the Atlanta Urban Design Commission, April 15, 1978) and "Moving into the Twenty-First Century" (more than
  80,000 burials as of 2017).
- Linda Stouffer, "Final resting place of John Lewis, Hank Aaron gains historic protection," WSB-TV, May 1, 2026 (added to
  the Georgia Register of Historic Places).
  <https://www.wsbtv.com/news/local/final-resting-place-john-lewis-hank-aaron-gains-historic-protection/ELFNFR2YKZF53PCSOIWOLLUH5U/>
- Georgia Historical Society marker, "South-View Cemetery" (2016): grown from 25 to more than 100 acres.
  <https://georgiahistory.com/ghmi_marker_updated/south-view-cemetery/>
